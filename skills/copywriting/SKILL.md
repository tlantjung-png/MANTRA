# Copywriting

## Use When

Use for product pages, landing pages, advertisements, email, social posts,
campaign concepts, and other copy intended to prompt an action. For rewriting
existing prose without changing its message, use `humanize`.

## Procedure

1. Identify the audience, offer, channel, desired action, voice, length, and
   factual support in the brief. Ask only for a missing fact that would change
   the claim or call to action; otherwise state a conservative assumption.
2. Choose one clear angle based on the audience's situation. Make the benefit
   concrete by connecting a supported feature to a practical consequence.
3. Draft for the channel. Use a hook, explanation, proof, and call to action
   only where each part helps the reader and the brief supports it.
4. Review every factual claim, number, comparison, testimonial, guarantee,
   scarcity cue, and price against the supplied evidence. Remove or qualify
   anything unsupported.
5. Read the result aloud for natural rhythm. Cut generic praise, repeated
   formulas, forced slang, and unnecessary calls to action. Produce variants
   only when they serve different audiences, channels, or useful angles.

## Verification

Deliver copy in the requested format. Briefly state any material assumption or
unverified claim that the user must supply before publishing.

## Boundaries

Do not invent product facts, customer evidence, results, or urgency. Do not
promise a detector score or claim guaranteed conversion. For financial or
trading copy, do not imply certain returns.
